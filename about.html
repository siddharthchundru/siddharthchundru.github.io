About

My work sits where two fields that rarely meet overlap: artificial life and embodied cognitive science, which have long theories of habit, homeostasis and self-directed learning, and modern robot learning, which has pretrained policies that need exactly those capacities. Most of my projects take an idea from the first and test, in a controlled experiment, whether it does real work in the second.

My background is in data science and cognitive science, and I'm currently pursuing a master's in data science at the University of Colorado Boulder. I also work on applied AI systems for clinical and rehabilitation settings, including speech and language modeling.

My independent research asks what a robot should practise when it improves itself, and whether recovering from a changed world and recovering from a changed body call for different mechanisms.

I also write about technology, cognition, and their broader economic and institutional contexts.
